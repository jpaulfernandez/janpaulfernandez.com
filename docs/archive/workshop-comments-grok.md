# Workshop landing: adversary notes (Grok)

I am not reviewing this as a designer or a copywriter. I am a stranger who found you and is trying to decide whether to give you money.

Who I am, for this pass: a Manila-based operator. I have sat through two vendor AI trainings this year. I have a real problem (team using ChatGPT in the shadows, no shared standard, leadership asking "what's our AI strategy"). I landed here from the homepage, or from the nav item labeled Workshops. I will give you about 90 seconds before I bounce to a LinkedIn course or a bigger-name consultant.

The page is `/workshops/`, not `/workshop`. If anyone types the singular, I assume they miss you.

---

## The first screen does not tell me what to do

I get a kicker (`Talks & workshops · Manila & Remote`), a headline (`AI workshops that end in something usable`), a lead, then **three equal links**:

- Open Cohorts (from ₱1,500)
- For Your Team (from ₱45,000)
- Start a conversation

That is not a landing. That is a table of contents.

"Something usable" is a slogan. Usable *what*? A prototype? A policy? A habit? The lead tries to list all three (`working prototypes, operational roadmaps, or team-wide standards`) and in doing so tells me you have not chosen who this page is for.

I still do not know:

- Can I buy a seat tonight?
- Are you teaching me, my staff, or a conference hall?
- Is this a product or a conversation?

Three links of equal weight means I have to do your segmentation for you. I will not. I will pick the cheapest number I can see (₱1,500) or I will leave.

**What would work:** one sentence that names the person and the outcome, then one primary action. The other audience can wait one scroll.

---

## You promised dates. There are no dates.

The availability strip says:

> Booking now
> September – October 2026
> One or two team engagements a month. Open Zoom cohort dates published below.

Today is 6 September 2026. So this window is *now*. Then I scroll to Open sessions.

There are no dates.

No Tuesday. No 7–9:30pm. No "next cohort 19 September, 6 seats left." The format line is `2.5 hours · Zoom · 25 seats`. That is a spec, not a schedule.

You told me dates were published below. They are not. That is the first time the page lies to me. After that, every other claim has to work harder.

Same problem on the homepage teaser: `September – October 2026 dates open`. Still no date.

**What would work:** an actual calendar row. Even one confirmed evening. If you do not have dates yet, do not say they are published below. Say "first cohort date goes up when 8 people ask" or take the strip down.

---

## I cannot buy anything

Every open-session row ends in **Reserve a seat**. That is a purchase verb. Clicking it does not reserve a seat. It scrolls me to a contact form titled **Start a conversation**.

I wanted a checkout. I got a sales call.

For ₱1,500 I expect: pick a date, pay GCash or a card, get a calendar invite. Instead you ask for my name, email, organisation, which SKU, headcount, timeline, and a free-text field labeled **What is going on**. Then a 30-minute call that "costs nothing."

That funnel is correct for a ₱140,000 on-site intensive. It is insulting for a ₱1,500 Zoom seat. You put the cheap product on the page to make the expensive one feel approachable, then you force the cheap product through the expensive product's sales motion. I will not fill a qualification form to buy a ticket.

The early-bird price makes this worse: `₱1,500 early bird · ₱2,500 regular`. When does early bird end? Is ₱1,500 the price I pay if I enquire today? Or is ₱1,500 already gone because it is September? I cannot tell, so I assume the number is decorative.

**What would work:** open seats are a product. Team work is a conversation. Two different last steps. If you refuse to take payment on the site, change the button to "Ask for the next date" and stop saying Reserve.

---

## Two buyers are fighting on one page, and the process section picks a side

You split Open sessions and For your team. Good instinct. Then **How it works** describes only the team path:

1. Discovery & Scope (30-minute call)
2. Pre-flight Check (IT, firewalls, sample data)
3. Hands-on Session (capped 12–20)
4. 30-Day Check-in

If I came for AI fluency at ₱1,500: I do not have a firewall problem. I do not need an intake questionnaire. I do not know if the 30-day check-in is included in my seat or only in the ₱90k workshop. The process quietly tells me the real customer is a company, and I am a decoy.

The enquiry copy does the same:

> Tell me what your people are actually stuck on.

I am one person. I do not have "people." The form is written for an HR lead who already decided to buy training.

Hero, open sessions, and the ₱1,500 price say I am welcome. Process, form, and "your people" say I am not. I will believe the form.

**What would work:** if open cohorts are real, give them their own how-it-works (register, Zoom link, show up, leave with X). If they are a top-of-funnel lure for team deals, be honest and stop pricing them like tickets.

---

## This is a catalog, not an offer

Nine things for sale:

Open: Fluency, Owners' table, Ship the idea.
Team: Talk, Deepfake keynote, Half-day, Full-day, Leadership, Shared standard.

That is a menu. A landing page is allowed one hero offer and a couple of variants. Nine SKUs with overlapping jobs means I have to become your salesperson and diagnose myself.

Specific collisions:

- **Talk or keynote** (from ₱45,000) vs **Keynote — disinformation, deepfakes and trust** (from ₱75,000). Is the second a specialty, or is the first a discount version of the same hour? If I am a conference organiser I do not know which email to send.
- **Half-day team workshop** (₱90,000, 20 people) vs **AI for leadership** (₱120,000, 12 executives). Leadership costs more for fewer people and a shorter day. Maybe that is correct. You never say why.
- **Full-day** (₱140,000) vs **Shared standard** (₱250,000, two sessions). The expensive one is the only one that names a deliverable I can put in a board deck (a policy). The cheaper ones name activities.

"From" on every team price is a shrug. From ₱45,000 to what? Travel? A second facilitator? VAT? I cannot take this to procurement. I also cannot tell if ₱90,000 is the number you actually want or the number you put on the internet to start a negotiation.

The walkouts on open sessions are the best copy on the page (`A practical daily workflow habit and a reliable framework for checking synthetic content`). The team rows have details, not walkouts. I walk out of a ₱140,000 day with… mapped operations? That is a facilitator describing their afternoon, not a buyer describing Monday.

**What would work:** pick a default. "Most teams start here: half-day, ₱90,000, you leave with N." Put the rest behind "other formats." Conferences get one keynote, not two.

## I do not believe you yet, and the page knows it

You have real material: Rappler, PHVote, two national elections, PSBank, MMDC, a data-handling policy that is more specific than most consultancies twice your size. I noticed. Then you spent it like a resume bullet instead of proof.

What is missing, in the order I looked for it:

1. **A face.** This is a personal site. The workshop page has no photograph of you, no still from a session, no room, no Zoom grid, no whiteboard. For a ₱1,500 individual purchase I am buying a person. I cannot see the person.
2. **Anyone who has done this.** Zero names. Zero companies. Zero "last Tuesday twelve PMs left with a prototype." Even "I have not run the public cohort yet; here is who I have trained inside companies" would be cleaner than silence. Silence reads as unlaunched.
3. **What the hours actually contain.** No agenda. No timed outline. No "minute 40 we try to break a deepfake." I have been burned by 'hands-on' that was a demo. Show me the rundown or I assume slides.
4. **What tools.** ChatGPT? Copilot? Claude? "Your tenant, your accounts" is good security language and useless shopping language. If my company only has Microsoft, is Owners' table still useful?
5. **A recording, a talk, a deck.** You sell keynotes from ₱45,000 with no clip. I will not put an unheard speaker in front of an all-hands.
6. **The Gmail.** `jpaul.fernandez18@gmail.com` next to ₱250,000 and a BIR/OR FAQ. Procurement will laugh. Individuals will still email it. You cannot charge institutional prices through a consumer inbox without looking like a side project. The FAQ says you can issue official receipts; the page never says you are a registered vendor. Those two facts should be in the same breath.
7. **After I send the form.** `/thanks` says you will get back "as soon as I can." I just asked about a ₱140,000 engagement. Tell me "weekday replies within one day" or I will keep shopping.

The privacy card is the most trustworthy block on the page. It is also below the fold, after I have already been asked to enquire nine times. Flip it. Corporate buyers bounce on data before they bounce on price.

The bio heading is `A practitioner, not a vendor trainer.` Then the paragraph talks like a vendor trainer (`practical capability`, `actionable policy standards`). I have read that sentence on twenty landing pages. The Rappler detail is the only thing I cannot get elsewhere. Lead with that. Drop the slogan.

## The FAQ answers attacks you invented, not questions I have

You open with:

> There are free AI courses online. Why invest in this?

I was not thinking that. I was thinking:

- When is the next Fluency session, in PHT?
- Do I get a refund if I cannot attend?
- English or Tagalog? (Manila & Remote does not say.)
- Do I need a ChatGPT Plus account, and who pays?
- Can I expense this? OR for ₱1,500 or only for companies?
- Will my competitors be in the Owners' table Zoom?
- Is the 30-day check-in included?
- What happens if IT blocks the tools on the day anyway?
- Can I see a sample agenda before I loop in my VP?

You did answer in-person vs Zoom, group size, IT restrictions, and official receipts. Those are good. They are the second-order questions. The first-order ones (when, pay how, what exactly, who else is in the room) are unanswered, so the FAQ feels like rebuttals to a blog comment, not help for a buyer.

## Discovery from the rest of the site is messy

How I found this:

- Homepage hero: three equal links, Workshops in the middle between writing and "Work with me."
- Homepage next section: a full workshops promo with two cards. Fine. Then later, **What I do** still lists Talks & Workshops as a service blob whose description (`product ideation, AI tools, and AI-assisted coding`) does not match the nine SKUs on `/workshops`.
- Nav: About, Writing, Projects, Gallery, Now, **Workshops**, Work with me. Workshops sits between concert photos and a second commercial page. I am not sure if this site is a person or a training company.
- `/work-with-me` also sells talks and workshops, with a different process (Discovery / Proposal / Hands-on) and a filled button. So there are two doors into the same house, with two different rituals.

If I am discovering you, I should hit one commercial door. Right now I can start a conversation in three places and I do not know which one is the real till.

The homepage cards also oversell. "View open seats & reserve" — there are no open seats to view. Seat caps are printed. Remaining seats are not.

## Small things that leaked trust while I was already doubtful

- **Reserve / Enquire / Start a conversation / Send it.** Four verbs, one action. I do not know what I am doing.
- **PMs, product managers** in the Ship the idea audience line. You said the same job twice. I read it as unedited.
- **How it works** is four identical cards. I have seen this on every SaaS site. It does not make me feel I am buying a session from a person who ran election night.
- **Frequently Asked Questions** as a heading. The rest of your site would have said Questions.
- **Organisation** and **Enquire** vs the rest of the site's American-leaning verbs (`prioritized`, `optimizing`). Tiny, but it adds to the "two authors" feeling.
- No mention of language, timezone, or what "Remote" means if I am in Cebu or Dubai.
- No images anywhere on a page selling a live event. I am being asked to imagine the room. I will imagine a generic Zoom training, because that is what I already know.

## What is actually working (so you do not throw it out)

I should be fair, or the rest sounds like noise.

You published prices. Almost nobody in this category does. That made me stay for the second scroll.

Seat caps (10, 12, 25, 20) made me take you more seriously than a "workshop for your whole company."

The open-session walkouts are concrete. Fluency especially: a daily habit plus a way to check synthetic content. I can repeat that to a colleague.

The data baseline (your tenant, sanitized data, zero retention, outputs are yours) is the one block I would forward to IT.

Splitting ₱1,500 seats from ₱45k+ engagements so the cheap number does not anchor the expensive one is correct. The execution just lets them contaminate each other anyway, via the shared form and the shared process.

You are not hiding that you are one person with limited capacity. "One or two team engagements a month" is the right kind of scarcity, if the dates were real.

---

## If I am the one discovering you: why I leave

I leave because I cannot complete a purchase, I cannot see a date, I cannot see you, and I cannot tell which of nine products is mine without a call I have not agreed to.

The page is confident about positioning and shy about the sale. It talks like a studio that has been running cohorts for two years. It behaves like a draft that is not allowed to take money yet.

I do not need more philosophy about vendor trainers. I need:

1. A date I can put in a calendar, or an honest "dates not up."
2. One recommended starting offer per audience (person vs team).
3. A way to pay for the cheap thing without a discovery call.
4. Proof a human has done this in a room.
5. An agenda I can screenshot into a Slack thread to justify the expense.
6. A reply-time and an invoice path that does not look like a side hustle.

Until those exist, I will keep you in a tab and buy the thing that lets me click Pay.

---

## Priority, if you only fix five things

1. **Stop promising dates you have not published.** Either put one on the page or delete the sentence.
2. **Stop saying Reserve a seat.** You cannot reserve. Align the verb with the form, or put a real checkout on open cohorts.
3. **Pick a default offer.** Hide the rest of the catalog behind it.
4. **Write How it works twice, or only for the audience the form actually serves.** Right now individuals are bait.
5. **Put proof above the form.** Face, agenda, one named outcome from a real session, reply SLA. Privacy can stay; it just cannot be the only specific thing.

I would not send this page to my boss. I would send it to a friend with "prices are honest, but I couldn't tell when it runs." That is the review you are one edit away from, and also the review that does not convert.
